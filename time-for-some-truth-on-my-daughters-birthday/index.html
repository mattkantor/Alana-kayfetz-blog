Time for some truth on my daughters birthday.

Here is the truth: I am scared of my daughter getting older.

Essa turns eight tomorrow. My only daughter, with three brothers, she holds a golden spot in my heart. And this birthday is hitting both of us harder than I expected.

This week, she came to me weeping.

“Mommy, I don’t want to get older. I want to stay seven forever.”

Then she said, “I already miss my childhood.”

That left me in puddles. Because I miss it too, even though we are right in the middle of it. I’m already yearning for moments that are still happening. She is here, wanting me, telling me things, asking me to come and watch. And somehow I’m missing her already.

She feels big. She loves big. She thinks big. Sometimes I hear what’s going on in that little head of hers and forget that she is still only seven.

I wonder if part of my fear is knowing some of the hardness that comes with being a girl, and then a woman. I don’t know how much of that she senses. But when she tells me she already misses her childhood, I want to pull her close and tell her there is still so much of it left.

There are monkey bars to hang from. Cartwheels to practise. New hobbies to try, get completely obsessed with, and maybe abandon a month later. She has time. I want her to feel that.

I daydream about her future, of course. I wonder who she will become and what she will love. But I know she will surprise me. She already does.

So I’m trying to meet her where she is today.

She wants to sing, dance and act? Okay, let’s go. She wants to make videos and dreams of a YouTube channel? Let’s figure it out together. She wants to spend the weekend hunting for squishies? I’m driving.

These things matter to her. I want to give them room, and I want to be there while she discovers what she likes.

For as long as she wants to roll with me, I am happily coming along. Her number one fan, her driver, the person she can ask to watch something “one more time.”

I know the tween years are coming. I know we won’t always be this easy together, and there will be days when she wants space from me. I hope I can give her that without making her responsible for how much I miss her.

But tonight, she is seven. Tomorrow, she will be eight.

I’m so proud of the girl she is becoming. I’m also having a hard time letting go of seven. Both are true, and I needed to say it.

Happy birthday, my sweet girl. There is still so much childhood ahead of you. And I’m here for all of it.
