Why my fitness has suddenly taken centre stage.

If you’ve noticed me talking more about fitness lately, there is a reason. And I haven’t quite known how to tell you.

In the middle of July, ten days after her 80th birthday, my mom had a severe stroke.

My mom, Carolyn, is sunshine. If you know her, you know. She is our queen, our matriarch, the woman everyone wants to sit beside. She loves, gives, helps and serves. She is beautiful and gracious, but also the coolest and the most fun. The kind of mom, bubbie and mother-in-law you feel incredibly lucky to have.

And suddenly, we were in the ICU, wondering whether we would get to keep her.

This wasn’t the first time.

When my mom was 48, she had a devastating heart attack. She nearly died, and the complications resulted in her losing both legs below the knee. I was ten years old.

For the next 32 years, she lived the fullest life. Truly. She has a zest that makes you want to be near her. She has dealt with so much illness, so many physical limitations, and still made room for other people, for fun, for beauty, for joy.

Then came this summer.

She is home now, slowly recovering, and needs care 24 hours a day. So much of the mom I know is still there. But her ability to move and do things for herself has changed enormously. Watching her want to do things her body cannot currently manage is heartbreaking.

I am hopeful. I am also scared.

Right now, we’re paying attention to what brings her pleasure. What makes her smile. How she can still give love and receive it. Those moments matter so much when so much else has been taken out of your hands.

And somewhere in all of this, I started looking at my own body differently.

I am now only about five years younger than my mom was when her life first changed so dramatically. Growing up, our almost 40-year age gap felt enormous. Now, 48 feels very close.

That realization hit me hard.

I want to enter the next decades with as much strength and mobility as I can build. If I ever need to recover from an illness or injury, I want to give myself the strongest starting point I can.

I know I cannot exercise my way into a guarantee. My mom’s health challenges were never a failure of effort or spirit. But watching what recovery asks of her has made me take my own health seriously in a way I hadn’t before.

So I am making time. Moving my body. Paying attention to how I feed it. Following through even when life is full and there are a hundred other things asking for me.

Yes, I want to look better. I’m enjoying feeling better, too. I’m allowed to want that.

But when I think about why I’m doing this, I think about being able to get up, go out, join in. I think about my children getting older and the life I still want to share with them. I think about having the strength to keep doing the things that bring me pleasure.

My mom has taught me so much about living joyfully in a body that has been through hell. This summer has also taught me how much I want to care for mine while I have the opportunity.

So if you see me sharing my journey to “shred,” this is what’s underneath it.

I’m in this for the long haul. I want a full life, and I want to be as physically able to enjoy it as I can.

With love,
Alana
