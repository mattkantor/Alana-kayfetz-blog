I am in Shock. My 8 weeks transformation

I recently looked at my two-month progress photos from the Forty Forward program, and I was genuinely shocked.

So shocked that my first instinct was not to share them.

Before-and-after photos can be complicated. They can be motivating, but they can also be triggering. I never want to suggest that the body on the left was bad and the body on the right is good. Both bodies are mine. Both deserve respect.

I have lived in a larger body for my entire life. I have worn everything from an XL to pants ranging between sizes 12 and 18. A size 10 on my wedding day was probably the smallest I had ever been, and if I’m being honest, I’m fairly certain I got there in an unhealthy way.

So seeing such a noticeable change after two months of strength training felt almost unbelievable. But the most important transformation is not the one you can see in the photos.

This week, my husband said something that stayed with me. He told me he has loved and been attracted to my body at every size, 20 pounds up, 20 pounds down and everywhere in between.

What he finds especially attractive right now is my commitment.

He is watching me dedicate time to myself. He sees me getting up, training, making choices with intention and keeping promises to my own body.

That is what this chapter is really about.

When I look directly at my deepest fears, I know my mother’s recent illness and stroke have forced me to confront what it was like to grow up with a very sick mom.

My mom, Carolyn, had a massive heart attack at 47 years old (4 years away from my current age). She was in a coma for three months. She developed gangrene and ultimately lost both of her legs below the knee.

I was 11.

She has been seriously unwell for most of my life. Not occasionally sick. Not minorly unwell. Her health shaped my childhood and, in ways I am still understanding, shaped me.

Yesterday, my ten-year-old looked at me out of nowhere and asked, “If you had three wishes, what would you wish for?”

I told him that for myself, I would wish for health. For our family, health and wealth. And for the world, peace.

He thought for a moment and then asked, “If you had two extra wishes, would you wish for Bubbie to grow her legs back and not be sick right now, and also make Henry healthy?”

I was dumbfounded.

His insight, his empathy and his understanding of what our family carries broke something open in me. We cried together and had a very real conversation about things I wish a ten-year-old did not have to understand so deeply.

And suddenly, all of this became very clear.

I am not strength training because I am chasing the smallest version of myself.

I am doing this because I want to give my children a healthy mom.

I want to age with strength. I want to move well. I want to do everything reasonably within my control to care for this body while I have the opportunity.

I also think I am trying to heal the 11-year-old inside me who learned far too early that a mother’s health can change everything.

It saddens me beyond words that fear and painful family experiences have become part of my motivation. But they are not the whole story.

Love and my children motivating me. The possibility of a long, strong and full life is motivating me.

The physical changes are exciting, and I am proud of them. But what I am most proud of is my dedication. For two months, I have shown up for myself in a way I’m not sure I ever have before.

And I’m beginning to believe something I should have known all along:

No shit, I can do this.

I can become stronger without hating the body I started in. I can be proud of changing while still honouring every version of myself that got me here.

If you are standing at the beginning of your own chapter, let my story remind you that it is not too late to choose yourself.

Your body does needs to become smaller to be worthy, but because your life is worth being strong enough to live.
