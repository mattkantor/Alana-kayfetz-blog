If every decision I made were truly my own, what would I choose?

Lately, I have been thinking about the questions we ask ourselves when the life we built no longer fits quite the way it once did.

There is a particular kind of pressure that comes with being the person responsible for making payroll. People see the business, the flexibility and the success. They do not always see the nights spent doing the math, carrying the livelihoods of other people and wondering how long you can keep holding it all.

Sometimes, if I am being completely honest, I want to burn the whole thing down.

Not because I don’t love what I have built. I do. But I have started wondering how much of it I am still consciously choosing.

I recently read two essays from Brianna Wiest’s 101 Essays That Will Change the Way You Think. One asks us to imagine what the life we want would look like on an ordinary day. The other reminds us that our lives are not one continuous story we are required to keep following. We are allowed to close a chapter, change the plot and become someone new.

Those ideas left me with some uncomfortable questions:

If every decision I made were truly my own, what would I choose?

Not the decision that looks impressive. Not the one that makes the most sense to everyone else. Not what a successful woman, responsible mother or ambitious business owner should do.

Would I keep growing the business? Make it smaller? Change the offer? Build a bigger team? Release parts of it? Work less? Earn differently? Begin again?

And if I already had the life I say I want, what would an ordinary Tuesday actually look like?

What time would I wake up? How would I feel in my body? Who would receive my energy? What would I work on? What would I finally stop doing?

Maybe wanting to burn it all down does not mean we want to quit. Maybe it means a part of us is asking whether the life we created still belongs to us.

We do not have to reject everything we have built to choose differently. We can be grateful for the chapter that brought us here without forcing ourselves to remain in it forever.

So this is the question I am sitting with:

What would my life and business look like if I stopped making decisions based on what I should do and started choosing what feels truly mine?
